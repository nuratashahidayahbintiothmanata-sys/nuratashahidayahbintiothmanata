Application of Derivatives in Food Security

Modeling Oil Palm (*Elaeis guineensis*) Fruit Bunch Development for Optimal Harvest

The Bioscience Problem

In Malaysia, optimizing the Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) yield of oil palm is vital for sustainable food security and bio-economy. Harvesting too early results in low oil content, while harvesting too late leads to fruit dropping and rancidity.

By monitoring a fruit bunch over its 20-week development cycle, we model its weight accumulation using a Logistic Growth Function. The first derivative $f'(x)$ tells us the exact rate of weight gain per week, pinpointing when the fruit enters its most rapid development phase.

Mathematical Modeling

Biomass Function $f(x)$: f(x) = L / (1 + e^(-k(x - x0))) Calculates cumulative weight (kg) at week $x$.
First Derivative $f'(x)$ (Growth Rate): f'(x) = [L * k * e^(-k(x-x0))] / (1 + e^(-k(x-x0)))^2 Calculates the rate of weight gain (kg/week) at week $x$.

Parameters (Based on MPOB guidelines):

• $L$ (Max Weight) = 25.0 kg

• $k$ (Growth Modifier) = 0.45

• $x_0$ (Inflection Point) = 10.0 weeks

Data Framework & Reference:

Inspired by standard physiological growth curves published by the Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB) via the "MPOB Palm Oil Yield and Growth Physiology Data Webpage".
